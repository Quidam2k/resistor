### FOIA / read-side & strategy — surfaced 2026-09-05 (source: `notes/2026-09-05-foia-strategy-source-doc.md`)
_Reframe: Resistor is the **write-side** (turns opinion into pressure); FOIA is the **read-side** (compels facts nobody wants published). A letter carrying a primary-source document a staffer has never seen is intelligence delivery, not advocacy. All CAPTURE-ONLY — plan-back before building any. Same precision ethic as letters: no request floods, no fabricated citations (Shepardize or don't file)._
9. **FOIA deadline tracker** — `proposed`. **Highest value-per-line; build first if we build one.** Passive: 20-business-day statutory clock + appeal deadlines + tolling events per request. "Agency X is 47 days past deadline on request 24-1183, here's your appeal draft." Legally meaningful, agencies blow it constantly, almost nobody tracks it. Suggested home: `src/foia/tracker.py`.
10. **regulations.gov comments as a delivery channel** — `proposed`. **Biggest strategic gap, FOIA or not.** A substantive comment on a proposed rule creates a *binding APA obligation* (agency must respond in the final rule; failure = grounds for challenge) — more legal weight than 1,000 letters. Reuses the research→compose→fact-check→deliver machinery; regulations.gov has a public API. Composition prompt changes (raise specific factual/legal defects, not preference).
11. **Committee/markup timing model** — `proposed`. **Highest-ROI small change per the doc.** Floor votes are the *worst* moment (positions locked); leverage is pre-markup, appropriations request/markup cycle, pending nominations, open comment periods, recess. Add `committees` to `representatives.yaml`, pull committee assignments + scheduled markups; open a session with "your rep sits on the committee holding HR 2201 — markup likely in 3 weeks."
12. **FOIA a rep's agency correspondence** — `proposed`. **Killer feature — VERIFY FIRST.** Congress isn't FOIA-able, but agencies are, and they hold letters members send them. Reveals what a member pushes for privately vs. publicly. **Must verify the "congressional control" doctrine + Exemption 6 case law before promising it in any README** — works, but not universally.
13. **Shared DOCUMENT corpus (not shared letters)** — `proposed`. Correct shape for the old Phase D. Coordinating *letters* = astroturf failure mode. Instead: a district-level shared collection of public records everyone drafts their own letter from. Gets coordination (50 letters citing real records) without detection risk or a user-data privacy problem.
14. **LLM-tell fingerprint check** — `proposed`. Worst failure mode is letters that read as AI output — em-dash cadence, tricolons, "It's not just X, it's Y." Staffers pattern-match on it now. Add an explicit anti-tell style constraint in the composition prompt + a fingerprint check in the fact-check pass. (Reinforces [[feedback_letter_style_no_ai_tells]].)
15. **Outcome-loop tracking (extend #2)** — `proposed`. We track sent letters + logged responses but not whether the *ask* worked over time. Log the ask, then check the subsequent vote/cosponsorship/public statement against it, so the "more effective" claim is falsifiable. Builds on the existing `asks` table.
16. **Vendor `unitedstates/congress` (CC0) as durable vote source** — `proposed`. Replaces the current GovTrack dependency (deprecated upstream 2017) and the non-existent Congress.gov member-votes endpoint. Bill text + House votes stay on Congress.gov; Senate roll calls from senate.gov XML. (P0 healthcheck added 2026-09-05 so a dead source can't be silently trusted meanwhile.)
17. **FOIA reconnaissance/nomenclature/denial-triage helpers** — `proposed` (lower priority). Cluster agency FOIA request logs (themselves FOIA-able) to avoid duplicate/bad requests; mine org charts + records schedules for exact record names before filing; parse denials against DOJ OIP guidance to surface unappealed improper denials. Multi-jurisdiction comparison (one narrow request to N agencies, normalize responses) is the underexploited move.
